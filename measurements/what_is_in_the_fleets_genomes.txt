THREE OF THESE FOUR TABLES SAY THE OPPOSITE OF WHAT THEY SAY WITHOUT THE CONTROL,
and the first version of this script had no control. Recorded because the wrong
readings are the intuitive ones.

TIME CONSTANTS: NOT CONVERGENCE. Zero fast neurons and zero slow ones looks like
a population that has settled on one way of using time. The seeded controls have
zero of each too. breed:random/1 draws genes uniform on plus or minus 8192, a
quarter of the 16-bit range, which puts every seeded tau in [0.406, 0.644]. That
is 25% of the expressible [0.05, 1.0), and the fleet has barely left it: the
widest champion reaches 0.382 and 0.657.

**The archipelago has never once flown a drone with a fast reflex or a long
memory.** Not because breeding rejected them. Because seeding cannot draw one and
mutation moves tau by about 0.0087 per nudge on one gene in twenty. Whether the
band is drift-limited or actively held there by selection is NOT answered here.

AND THE CONFINEMENT MATTERS, WHICH HAD TO BE CHECKED SEPARATELY. The CfC update
flown here is f = Sum/tau, gate = sigmoid(-f) with f clamped to [-10, 10], and
state = gate*state + (1-gate)*head. Tau is a DIVISOR ON THE INPUT, not a leak
rate, so it decides nothing while the weighted sum is large enough to pin the
sigmoid. A hidden neuron sums 41 inputs through weights of order 1, which made
"the gate is pinned and tau is inert" the obvious guess, and it would have made
the whole tau finding a non-issue.

It is wrong. Only 20-28% of hidden states sit at the bound, mean |state| is about
0.62, and all 24 neurons move on every island. The gate is live roughly three
quarters of the time, so tau is operating where its value changes behaviour, and
a quarter of its range is where the fleet has been kept.

SATURATION: NOT A SIGNATURE OF TRAINING. The champions straddle the controls,
beam00 below both and msi00 above. Random weights of this magnitude saturate tanh
just as hard. Note also that the inputs are closed-form, not sensor readings, so
this measures the network and not the flight.

WEIGHTS: MUTATION IS WORKING, AND THE HISTOGRAM CANNOT SAY MORE. Champions reach
-2.85 and 2.57 against a seeding box of exactly plus or minus 2.000, so genes have
been pushed 25-40% beyond anything seeding can produce. Mean |w| is unchanged at
about 1.0. That is the expected shape: selection acts on the ARRANGEMENT of
weights, which a marginal histogram is nearly blind to. Nothing here is evidence
that breeding has or has not improved anything.

KINSHIP: THE ONE POSITIVE RESULT, AND RAIDS ARE VISIBLE IN IT. Two genomes known
to be unrelated sit at 1.330, and every champion-to-seed pair agrees, averaging
1.348 over ten pairs. Every one of the ten champion-to-champion pairs is BELOW
that, averaging 1.239, with msi00 and beam00 closest at 1.146.

The five islands share ancestry. This is the first evidence that the raid
protocol moves genetic material and not merely counters: captures were already
counted in the thousands, and a counter cannot tell a genome that was absorbed
from one that was absorbed and immediately outcompeted.

It is a WEAK mixing, about 8% of the way from unrelated toward identical, and it
rests on one genome per island rather than the population.
