THE LINEAGES ARE DEEP, WHICH KILLS THE OBVIOUS EXPLANATION. Median generation is
106 to 158 and almost nothing sits at generation zero. The champions looked close
to seeded genomes and the tempting reading was that the population churns faster
than it adapts, so an entry is only ever a few mutations old. It is not so. These
are lineages 150 generations deep and they still have seeded time constants.

THE TAU BAND IS A POPULATION FACT. Not one of the 10,848 time constants in the
archipelago is fast (below 0.2) or slow (above 0.8). Between 2% and 7% have
escaped the band seeding drew them from at all. **No drone in the history of this
archipelago has had a fast reflex or a long memory, and none is on course to.**

AND THE REASON IS ARITHMETIC, NOT SELECTION. One mutation is a draw at sigma=600
gene units, on one gene in twenty.

  for a WEIGHT  600/4096          = 0.146 weight units, against a seeded spread
                                    of [-2, 2], so 3.7% of the range per nudge
  for a TAU     600 * 0.95/65536  = 0.0087 tau units, against an expressible
                                    0.95, so 0.9% of the range per nudge

Weights are seeded ACROSS the range they use, so a gene already sitting near 2.0
crosses the line on its first nudge, and every entry on every island carries at
least one weight outside the box. Time constants are seeded into the MIDDLE
QUARTER, so a tau must cross 0.206 units of dead ground before it is even fast.

At one gene in twenty over 150 generations each tau has taken about 7.5 nudges,
giving a random walk of sd 0.0087*sqrt(7.5) = 0.024. Reaching 0.2 is 8.6 standard
deviations. Reaching it at ONE standard deviation needs about 560 nudges, so
about 11,000 generations. The fleet is at 150. **The search would need roughly
seventy times its whole history to sample the range the encoding offers.**

Two quantities with different scales share one step size and one seeding draw.
That is the defect, and it is in `breed:random/1` and `?SIGMA` together, not in
`to_tau/1`, which is the only part that states its intent.
